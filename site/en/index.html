Counterexamples to Conjectures 3 and 4 of Huang, Stoica, Xia and Zhong (arXiv:2006.06716v1, Section 5.2, printed page 32)

Haobo Ma, Wenlin Zhang

Paper

Can local balance rules force every edge of an infinite branching network to have the same length? Haobo Ma and Wenlin Zhang answer no for two conjectures of Huang, Stoica, Xia and Zhong: their nonuniform positive lengths satisfy the rules even though every junction away from one distinguished path has equal incident lengths.

A tree is a connected graph without cycles. The infinite regular tree T_q has q+1 edges meeting each vertex; here q is any integer at least two. Assign positive edge lengths. At each vertex, divide the sum of reciprocal incident lengths by the sum of their squares, calling the result its effective local length R. Equal incident lengths give that same R. The equations, from earlier work on graph versions of Einstein equations, require an edge between vertices i and j to have length ℓ=(R_i²+R_j²)/(R_i+R_j). Since R depends on neighboring lengths, this is a coupled consistency problem.

The authors choose a path extending infinitely in both directions, called the spine, with vertices indexed by integers n. Fix a positive scale t and a positive multiplier r≠1. At vertex n, attach q−1 infinite branching trees, each with constant edge length t rⁿ, including its attachment. Give the spine edge from n to n+1 length a t rⁿ, where a=(r²+1)/(r+1). Every vertex then has the required degree. For q=r=2 and t=1, branch lengths are 2ⁿ and spine lengths are (5/3)2ⁿ: an attachment of length 1 sits beside a spine edge of length 5/3.

Why does this work? At spine vertex n, the choice of a makes the normalized contributions to the two sums defining R match, giving R=t rⁿ. Inside each attached branch, equal lengths give the same R. Branch edges therefore join equal effective lengths and satisfy the equation automatically. A spine edge joins effective lengths differing by factor r, and the chosen a satisfies its equation. These written calculations cover every edge of the infinite tree, not just a sampled neighborhood.

More generally, start with any positive solution on a connected, locally finite tree having an edge and degrees at most q+1. Fill each vertex’s missing neighbors with constant-length branches, using its original R as their length. Each added edge contributes 1/R to the reciprocal sum and 1/R² to the squared-reciprocal sum, preserving their ratio. Old equations remain valid; new edges satisfy them automatically. Applying this extension principle to Huang and colleagues’ earlier classification on a doubly infinite line gives spine lengths whose preceding-to-following ratios can independently be 2 or 3. The choices may be periodic or nonperiodic.

The cubic example refutes Conjecture 4, which asserted constancy on T_2. Every construction refutes Conjecture 3 for q≥2: equal incident lengths at one vertex do not force global equality. A valid global replacement is that a positive solution on any connected, locally finite tree with an edge has constant lengths exactly when all vertex ratios R agree. Gubser and collaborators and Huang and colleagues already constructed nonconstant solutions for odd q. The present work also covers even q, without requiring the incident edges to split into two equally sized groups.

The geometry is nonuniform in another sense. Using the earlier work’s edge-curvature definition, the authors show that an attachment edge has strictly greater curvature than an edge wholly inside a branch. In the cubic example these values are −13/21 and −2/3. Thus these equations do not force constant curvature; the examples differ from the constant-curvature metrics on finite trees mentioned in the manuscript.

The limits matter. For r≠1, spine lengths approach zero in one direction and grow without bound in the other; r=1 recovers the constant solution. These examples do not meet the earlier asymptotically constant boundary hypothesis or settle general scale-pattern classification. The authors report Lean evidence for the single infinite cubic counterexample, not the general family or extension principle. Finite exact-rational checks illustrate selected parameters, neighborhoods, periodic patterns and curvature formulas, but do not prove infinite statements. The general results have written proofs; the flexible-pattern result also relies on the prior line classification. The contribution is a failure of local-to-global rigidity, not a classification of all solutions.
